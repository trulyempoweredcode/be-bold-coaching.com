Am I Wasting My Life?

28 July 2026 Karlina Jordan

There comes a point for many of us when we quietly start wondering whether life is slipping through our fingers.


Perhaps it's your birthday and another year has passed more quickly than you expected. Perhaps a friend announces they're engaged, another has their first baby, someone else buys a house or gets the promotion they've been working towards. Without really noticing it, you begin taking stock of your own life. Suddenly, instead of seeing everything you've achieved, your attention lands on what isn't there yet. And you ask:


"Am I wasting my life?"


It's one of the most common questions I hear from clients in their twenties. Not because they're lazy or unmotivated, but because they're often working incredibly hard while carrying the constant feeling that they should somehow be further ahead.


The strange thing is that today you're the youngest you'll ever be again, yet it rarely feels that way. Instead, it can feel as though you're already running out of time. It's as though everyone else received a handbook explaining exactly when life is supposed to happen, and somehow yours got lost in the post.


We often tell ourselves pressurising things such as: you should have the career, the relationship, the house, maybe children. You should feel confident, settled and certain about the future. And if even one of those things isn't in place, especially while watching friends appear to have everything figured out, it's easy to conclude that you're falling behind.


The difficulty is that your mind rarely compares your whole life with someone else's whole life. It compares the one thing you're worried about with the one thing they seem to have. You notice their relationship while forgetting the career you've built. Or maybe you notice their fitness while overlooking your close friendships. You notice their confidence while ignoring your kindness, resilience or everything you've overcome to get where you are today.


We even know this isn't a fair comparison. We know social media shows highlights rather than ordinary life. We know there are people who would happily swap places with us. We know there are people behind us as well as ahead of us. Yet none of that seems to matter because the feeling remains.


One of the reasons, I think, is that the voice telling you you're wasting your life isn't actually trying to hurt you. It's trying to protect you.
When clients describe this voice, it usually sounds something like this: "You're falling behind." "Everyone else has worked it out." "You're running out of time." On the surface it sounds critical, but underneath it is often something much softer. It's a frightened part of the mind desperately trying to make sure you don't wake up twenty years from now full of regret.


The intention is actually quite beautiful. This part of you wants you to have a meaningful life. The problem is that it has mistaken anxiety for motivation.
Unfortunately, self criticism rarely creates momentum. Imagine trying to motivate a friend by reminding them every day that they were failing. Before long they would begin to believe it themselves. They wouldn't feel inspired. They'd feel defeated. Yet many of us speak to ourselves in exactly this way, assuming harshness will somehow produce discipline when, more often than not, it simply produces paralysis.
The more ashamed we feel, the smaller our world becomes. We avoid, we procrastinate, we compare ourselves even more. Ironically, the very voice trying to stop us wasting our lives often makes it harder to enjoy the life we're already living.


One question I often ask clients is surprisingly simple.

Where is your time actually going?

If you wish you exercised more, where is your time going? If you feel guilty for not travelling, where is your time going? If you believe you're wasting your twenties, where is your time going?


Very often the answer is work. Or studying. Or caring for family. Or recovering from something painful. I recently spoke with a client who had just received a promotion after years of hard work, yet all they could focus on was the relationship they didn't have and the holidays they hadn't taken. Their success had become invisible because their attention was fixed entirely on what was missing.


Perhaps that's one of the biggest myths we absorb: that every part of life should progress at the same pace. We imagine we're supposed to build a career, meet the right person, stay fit, travel, buy a home, nurture friendships and discover our purpose all at once. But life rarely unfolds like that. Sometimes we're building. Sometimes we're healing. Sometimes we're surviving. Sometimes we're simply learning who we are.


So if you've found yourself wondering whether you're wasting your life, perhaps there's a gentler question to ask instead.
What am I actually building right now?
Because wherever you've been consistently investing your time, your energy and your attention, something has been growing. It may not be the thing you've been measuring yourself against, but that doesn't make it any less valuable.


Life isn't measured by how many milestones you've reached by a certain birthday. It's measured by whether you're gradually building a life that feels meaningful to you. And those are two very different things.
